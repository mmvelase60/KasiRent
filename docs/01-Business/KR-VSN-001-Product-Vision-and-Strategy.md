## Vision

Give small landlords a clear, trustworthy record of their rooms, rent and payments without requiring them to adopt agency-style processes. A landlord should be able to answer: who occupies each room, what has been charged, what has been received and what remains outstanding.

## Users and jobs

The first user is an owner or family member managing roughly 1–30 rooms. That range is a product focus, not an enforced subscription limit. Their work includes registering renters, receiving cash or EFT, following up partial payments and finding historical records during a discussion.

Renters benefit from clear receipts and corrections, but do not sign into the current app. A multi-staff property manager and a renter portal are later segments.

## Product principles

- Preserve money history. Correct mistakes visibly.
- Use plain terms: room, monthly rent, amount received, balance.
- Record cash and EFT with equal clarity.
- Make empty, disconnected and unsaved states explicit.
- Collect personal details only for an identified purpose.
- Add complexity only where an observed workflow requires it.

## Differentiation hypothesis

The opportunity is a focused room-rental workflow that users can learn quickly. Existing rental platforms mean feature novelty alone is not a sufficient advantage. Ease of onboarding, trusted balances and low support effort should be tested in use.

## Business model hypotheses

A room-count subscription is proposed. R49 per month for up to 10 rooms is an experiment inherited from the initial discussion, not a market-validated price or committed offer. Free tenant access could accompany a future portal. No subscription enforcement or billing exists.

Track activation as a landlord creating a property, room, tenancy, charge and payment. Track continued use as records updated over two consecutive rent cycles. Proposed pilot targets: five participating landlords, at least four completing onboarding and at least three returning for a second cycle. These are goals, not results.

## Strategic exclusions

Do not turn the first release into a room marketplace, deposit wallet, credit bureau or automated legal advice product. Translation, payment integrations and advanced analytics need their own evidence and requirements.
